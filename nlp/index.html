How does a computer understand words?

NLP means Natural Language Processing: teaching computers to work with human language.

Everything here uses a real model: Google's pretrained word2vec (learned from Google News, 300 numbers per word), trimmed to about 8,000 words so it runs right in your browser.

How it learns: the company a word keeps

“You shall know a word by the company it keeps.” word2vec reads billions of sentences and nudges each word's numbers so it can guess its neighbors.

      Recap

      What we learned

      • 1
        Words become numbers. Each word is a list of 300 numbers.
      • 2
        Meaning is position. Those numbers are a location in “meaning space”.
      • 3
        Similar words cluster. Words used in similar company end up close together.
      • 4
        Directions are relationships. man → king is roughly the same step as woman → queen.

      Limits: the model learned from news text, so it also learned that text's biases. And each word gets just one vector, so “bank” (river) and “bank” (money) are mixed together.

      Newer AI like chatbots builds on the same idea, but gives each word different numbers depending on the sentence around it.
